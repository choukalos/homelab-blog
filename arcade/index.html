Asteroids

Atari · 1979

One of the most influential arcade games ever made. Atari's spin-off from the successful Asteroid/Kilobot prototype established the vector graphics standard for arcade games. Players control a single spaceship in an asteroid field, destroying rocks that break into smaller pieces. It defined the space shooter genre and became a cultural icon of the early 1980s arcade scene.

Galaga

Namco · 1981

Namco's masterpiece sequel to Galaxian introduced dive-bombing alien formations and a unique second-life mechanic. Players must defend against coordinated enemy attacks with three distinct alien types. Galaga's elaborate formation-based gameplay and increasing difficulty curve set the standard for fixed-shooters and remains one of the most beloved arcade classics.

Atari · 1977

Atari's deep-sea mining adventure puts you in control of a lone diver mining for diamonds on the ocean floor while fending off sharks, octopuses, and other sea creatures. The 4-player simultaneous gameplay made it a crowd favorite in arcades. Its unique premise combining resource collection with survival action influenced the underwater action genre for decades.

Elite

Firelight/Lucid Dreams · 1984

The groundbreaking space trading and combat sim that pioneered the open-ended sandbox genre. Created by Ian Bell and David Braben, Elite let players explore a procedurally generated galaxy of 8 galaxies and 256 star systems, trading goods, bounty hunting, and engaging in 3D wireframe dogfights. It won over 50 computer game awards and is widely considered one of the most important games ever made.

Missile Command

Atari · 1980

Atari's Cold War-themed defense game where players intercept incoming ICBMs with limited artillery. Designed by Dave Theurer, Missile Command's escalating difficulty and looming nuclear threat created unmatched tension for its era. Its game over screen became one of gaming's most haunting moments, and the game remains a powerful example of gaming as social commentary.

Tetris

Electronorgtechnika · 1984

Created by Soviet programmer Alexey Pajitnov, Tetris is the best-selling puzzle game in history. Inspired by the pentomino puzzle game, its simple mechanics of rotating falling blocks create endlessly challenging gameplay. Born during the Cold War and famously smuggled out of the USSR, Tetris became a global phenomenon and is widely regarded as one of the greatest games ever created.

Atari · 1979

Chris Saunders' physics-based landing simulator challenged players to use limited fuel to safely land a spacecraft on marked landing pads while avoiding craters and mountains. Originally released as a single-screen arcade game, its realistic physics and difficulty made it a cult favorite and inspired countless space-landing games and modern titles.

Pitfall!

Atari · 1982

Atari's 1982 jungle platformer, designed by Chris Crawford, became one of the best-selling video games of all time. Pat races across a circular world of connected screens, leaping crocodiles, dodging venomous snakes and spikes, and swinging from vines — with underground tunnel shortcuts you drop into and jump back out of. Collect all 10 gems before the 20-minute timer runs out; clear the final level and you get the abrupt 'kill screen' ending that made the 2600 version famous.

Kung Fu Master

Irem · 1984

Kung Fu Master (1984) was Irem's landmark side-view beat-'em-up and one of the genre's first — a lone martial artist in a white gi fights through waves of thugs and a boss to defend a temple. This browser version is a loving tribute to that classic: a scrolling hallway you battle your way down, with themed levels cycling through Temple, Dojo, Courtyard, Night Street, and Mountain Path. Punch and kick green and red brawlers, keep your distance from the rock throwers and nunchaku fighters, and take down the boss at the far end of each round. It's close to the original in spirit and mechanics, but not a pixel-perfect replica. Endless rounds with rising difficulty and a persistent hi-score.

Star Wars

Midway · 1983

Star Wars (1983) was Midway's landmark first-person shooter — you piloted an X-wing down the Death Star's surface trench in a cockpit view, dodging TIE-fighter laser fire and firing proton torpedoes at thermal exhaust ports. This browser version captures that classic feel: fly down a 3D-perspective trench from the cockpit, aim the crosshair, and blast thermal ports with your limited torpedo supply while dodging lasers from TIEs and wall turrets. Destroy enough ports to expose the Death Star's core, then blow it up to clear the run. Endless runs with rising difficulty and a persistent hi-score. It's close to the original in spirit and mechanics, but not a pixel-perfect replica.
